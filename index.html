01 · Premise

The thing beneath the thing.

On why most founder businesses break at the same place.
Working assumption

The bottleneck in a founder-led business is rarely the founder's expertise. It is the absence of a structure that lets the expertise travel.

Adjacent assumption

A learning experience is not content. It is a sequence of decisions a person practices until those decisions become reflexes.

Most founder-led businesses are held in place by one person's attention. The expertise is real. The clients are real. The work is real. What is missing is the architecture that lets any of it operate without the founder in the room.

That missing layer is not a brand, not a funnel, not a tech stack. It is the operating substrate — the curriculum a trainer can teach without you, the tool a player can run without supervision, the content engine that produces audience data while you sleep, the train-the-trainer layer that turns one of you into many.

Built carelessly, this layer becomes a graveyard of half-finished assets. Built deliberately, it becomes the part of the practice that compounds.

Most clients arrive asking for a course. The right answer is almost never a course.

02 · Operating Principles

Six lenses the work moves through.

Each principle resists a more popular instinct.
P.01

Start with the learner, not the content.

The right starting point is never the syllabus. It is the person, the decision they are unable to make yet, and the most direct path to making it become reflex. Content is the consequence of that path, not its origin.

Resists the impulse to package what you already know.

P.02

Design the system, not the asset.

A single course is fragile. A system — a learning path, a content engine, a train-the-trainer layer — keeps producing value after the build is done. The question is never what should we make; it is what should keep working.

Resists the deliverable-shaped brief.

P.03

Build so the founder can leave the room.

If the practice depends on the founder showing up, the practice is the founder. Every artifact is judged by a single test: can someone trained, not cloned, deliver this without losing what makes it work?

Resists the heroic founder model.

P.04

Let every artifact do more than one job.

A post is also a tool. A tool is also research. A research signal is also content for the next module. Anything built carries multiple loads — for the audience, for the practice, for the system's own learning about itself.

Resists the single-purpose deliverable.

P.05

Treat structure as care.

Structure is not what gets in the way of warmth. It is how warmth survives scale. The trainer who has clarity can be human in the room. The learner who has scaffolding can be brave inside it.

Resists the false choice between systems and soul.

P.06

Move at the pace of the practice, not the platform.

Launch dates are imposed; readiness is earned. The system is finished when the practice can operate it — not when the calendar says so. Tempo is a design choice, and the right tempo is almost always slower than the founder wants.

Resists the conversion-funnel calendar.

03 · System Architecture

Four layers, observed from below.

A reading of how a learning practice actually stands up.

Every learning business looks like a course from the outside. From the inside, it is four layers stacked. Most of the work happens in the bottom two — the layers no client ever asks for.

The diagram is not metaphor. It is the order in which the work is actually built. Skipping a layer is the most common reason a launch holds together for six weeks and then quietly stops.

DOSSIER · 01
01
Filed · 2021 — 2024
Built · Now operating without us
Johns Hopkins University · Center for Technology in Education

From slide decks to a six-state training system that runs without us in the room.

Educators across multiple states needed training on a complex assessment system. Every new cohort, every new hire, required the team to be there. The work could not scale without breaking the consistency it depended on.

What we constructed was a core training architecture with distinct learning paths for six states, wrapped in a train-the-trainer model and extended with asynchronous modules as states asked for more flexibility. The system flexed without losing its integrity.

SituationMulti-state training bottlenecked on a single team's presence.
ConstructedCore curriculum + six state-specific paths + train-the-trainer + async modules.
What persistsTrainers onboard staff independently. The team is no longer the bottleneck.

Operating · in iteration
The CoLLab Field Hockey

A content engine that grows the audience, builds the tool library, and scopes the future course at the same time.

Four hundred Substack subscribers. Deep expertise. No system beneath any of it. What was needed was a structure that built the audience consistently and produced the evidence a paid product would later require.

What we constructed is a thirty-two week content system across four coaching pillars. Every post serves two audiences at once — coaches reading the Substack and players running interactive drills before practice. A branded tool library compounds with each post; an audience data layer compounds underneath it.

DOSSIER · 03
03
Opened · 2026 — In development
In construction
Iris B Photography

Turning wildlife expertise into interactive experiences that teach animal behavior before the shot.

Wildlife photography instruction goes beyond technique. It depends on reading behavior in the field — a skill that cannot be taught from a static post. The learning has to feel like being there, and it has to fit on a phone in someone's hand.

What we are constructing is a growing library of mini learning experiences built around real field scenarios. The learner reads what an animal is doing, makes a decision, receives feedback connecting the behavior to the photographic moment. Short. Specific. Cumulative.

SituationField skill that cannot survive in a slide, an article, or a video.
ConstructingScenario-based behavior drills; a bundle-ready library for course or workshop use.
Will persistA modular asset base that can be sold, taught, or used as a lead instrument.

06 · The Architect

A single practitioner, by design.

No team. No agency layer. No proxy between you and the work.
Practitioner profile · Foundation Forward

Kimberly Lohr

Good design starts with one question: what does this person actually need to be able to do? Not what content needs to be covered. Not what format looks impressive. What does the learner need, and what is the most direct path to get them there?

That question leads to experiences that are more focused than people expect and more effective than they thought possible. It also leads to systems — ones that scale, hold more than one purpose at once, and keep working long after the build is done.

The work that matters most here is the work done with people who care deeply about what they are building. The coach who has spent twenty years refining her methodology. The director obsessed with getting it right for every child in her center. The photographer who sees what others miss and wants to teach others to see it too.

That passion is the raw material. The job of the practice is to build the system around it.
